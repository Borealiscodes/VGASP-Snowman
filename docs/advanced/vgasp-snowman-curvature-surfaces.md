🧩 1. Overview
Curvature surfaces are geometric surfaces defined by the Curvature Tensor \( K_{ij} \).  
They determine:

- where stacking is allowed  
- where rolling is stable  
- where decoration is safe  
- how dissipation interacts with geometry  
- how invariants couple to curvature

Curvature surfaces apply to all operators: roll, stack, decorate.

---

🧭 2. Types of Curvature Surfaces

⚪ 2.1. Positive Curvature Surfaces
Stable, convex surfaces suitable for stacking.

Operators must:

- align stack geometry  
- maintain curvature thresholds  
- preserve convexity

Operators must not:

- stack on curvature below stability threshold  
- decorate unstable convex surfaces  
- roll in ways that flatten curvature

---

🟣 2.2. Zero‑Curvature Surfaces
Flat surfaces with neutral stability.

Operators must:

- monitor dissipation coupling  
- maintain identity boundaries  
- avoid excessive pressure

Operators must not:

- stack tall structures  
- decorate with heavy geometry  
- roll across zero‑curvature discontinuities

---

🔵 2.3. Negative Curvature Surfaces
Concave surfaces with inherent instability.

Operators must:

- treat negative curvature as forbidden for stack  
- stabilize curvature before decoration  
- avoid roll amplification

Operators must not:

- stack on negative curvature  
- decorate concave instability zones  
- roll across negative curvature gradients

---

🌀 2.4. Curvature‑Invariant Surfaces
Surfaces defined jointly by \( K{ij} \) and \( I{ij} \).

Operators must:

- preserve identity‑curvature coupling  
- maintain symmetry curvature planes  
- respect invariant curvature boundaries

Operators must not:

- violate identity curvature surfaces  
- create curvature discontinuities  
- decorate across invariant curvature boundaries
