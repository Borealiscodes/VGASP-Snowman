🧩 1. Overview
Holonomy surfaces are geometric surfaces defined by the Holonomy Tensor \( H_{ijk} \).  
They determine:

- how orientation evolves  
- where orientation may change  
- where orientation must remain fixed  
- how operators interact with path‑dependent geometry

Holonomy surfaces apply to all operators: roll, stack, decorate.

---

🧭 2. Types of Holonomy Surfaces

🌀 2.1. Holonomy‑Lock Surfaces
Surfaces where orientation becomes fixed.

Operators must:

- finalize orientation before decoration  
- respect holonomy lock boundaries  
- maintain orientation invariants

Operators must not:

- roll after holonomy lock  
- stack during orientation drift  
- decorate before lock

---

🔄 2.2. Holonomy‑Drift Surfaces
Surfaces where orientation changes gradually.

Operators must:

- monitor drift rate  
- stabilize orientation before stack  
- avoid decoration during drift

Operators must not:

- stack across drift discontinuities  
- decorate unstable drift surfaces  
- roll in ways that amplify drift

---

🔁 2.3. Holonomy‑Loop Surfaces
Surfaces that define allowable closed orientation loops.

Operators must:

- maintain loop contractibility  
- preserve orientation symmetry  
- respect invariant loop boundaries

Operators must not:

- create non‑contractible loops  
- violate symmetry constraints  
- generate illegal holonomy cycles
