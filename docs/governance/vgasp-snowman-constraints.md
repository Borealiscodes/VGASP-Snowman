🧩 1. Overview
Governance constraints define what operators may do, what they must do, and what they must never do.

They are enforced by four canonical tensors:

- Invariant Tensor \( I_{ij} \) — identity + symmetry  
- Curvature Tensor \( K_{ij} \) — geometry + stability  
- Holonomy Tensor \( H_{ijk} \) — orientation + path‑dependence  
- Dissipation Tensor \( D_{ij} \) — melting + deformation

Governance constraints apply to all operators: roll, stack, decorate.

---

🧭 2. Global Governance Rules

🔒 2.1. Invariant Constraints
Invariants enforce identity and prevent illegal transformations.

Operators must:

- preserve symmetry  
- preserve identity surfaces  
- respect invariant boundaries  

Operators must not:

- create decorations before invariant surfaces exist  
- violate symmetry constraints  
- alter identity tensors

---

🌀 2.2. Curvature Constraints
Curvature governs geometry and stability.

Operators must:

- maintain curvature thresholds  
- align curvature during stacking  
- stabilize curvature before decoration  

Operators must not:

- stack on unstable curvature  
- roll after curvature is locked  
- decorate surfaces with negative curvature

---

🧭 2.3. Holonomy Constraints
Holonomy governs orientation and path‑dependent geometry.

Operators must:

- align orientation before stacking  
- respect holonomy trajectories  
- finalize holonomy before decoration  

Operators must not:

- decorate before orientation is fixed  
- roll after holonomy is locked  
- create holonomy loops that violate invariants

---

🔥 2.4. Dissipation Constraints
Dissipation governs melting, deformation, and energy loss.

Operators must:

- minimize dissipation during roll  
- stabilize dissipation before stack  
- avoid dissipation hotspots during decoration  

Operators must not:

- decorate melting surfaces  
- stack during active dissipation  
- roll when dissipation exceeds threshold \( D_{crit} \)
