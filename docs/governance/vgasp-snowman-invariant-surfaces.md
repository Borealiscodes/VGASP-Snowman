🧩 1. Overview
Invariant surfaces are geometric surfaces defined by the Invariant Tensor \( I_{ij} \).  
They determine:

- where operators may act  
- where operators must not act  
- how identity is preserved  
- how symmetry is enforced  
- how geometry remains stable

Invariant surfaces apply to all operators: roll, stack, decorate.

---

🧭 2. Types of Invariant Surfaces

🟦 2.1. Identity Surfaces
Defined by stable values of \( I_{ij} \).  
These surfaces preserve Snowman identity.

Operators must:

- respect identity boundaries  
- preserve identity symmetry  
- avoid altering identity tensors

Operators must not:

- decorate identity surfaces prematurely  
- roll across identity boundaries  
- stack across identity discontinuities

---

🔵 2.2. Symmetry Surfaces
Defined by symmetry constraints in \( I_{ij} \).

Operators must:

- maintain symmetry alignment  
- preserve canonical axes  
- respect symmetry planes

Operators must not:

- create asymmetric decorations on symmetry surfaces  
- stack off‑axis  
- roll in ways that break symmetry

---

⚪ 2.3. Curvature‑Invariant Surfaces
Defined by joint constraints from \( I{ij} \) and \( K{ij} \).

Operators must:

- maintain curvature thresholds  
- align stack geometry  
- decorate only stable curvature regions

Operators must not:

- stack on negative curvature  
- decorate unstable curvature  
- roll across curvature discontinuities

---

🌀 2.4. Holonomy‑Invariant Surfaces
Defined by constraints from \( I{ij} \) and \( H{ijk} \).

Operators must:

- finalize orientation before decoration  
- align holonomy paths  
- preserve orientation invariants

Operators must not:

- decorate before holonomy lock  
- roll after holonomy lock  
- create holonomy loops that violate invariants
