Abstract

Humans project themselves onto everything — tools, animals, weather systems, algorithms, and even geometric manifolds — because anthropomorphism is a cognitive shortcut for compressing complexity into familiar experiential structures. This preprint examines the human tendency toward projection, explains why anthropomorphic cognitive architectures are inherently limited, and argues for manifold‑based geometric cognition as a more advanced, non‑anthropomorphic paradigm for artificial intelligence.

We show that understanding anthropomorphism is necessary for designing geometric AI, but that anthropomorphic architectures are not the pinnacle of cognitive design. Instead, we propose advanced geometric directions grounded in multi‑manifold cognition, modal universes, spectral geometry, drift‑curvature coupling, holonomy bundles, and projection‑surface epistemics.

1. The Human Condition: Why We Project Ourselves Onto Everything

Humans evolved with:

- subjective experience  
- embodied cognition  
- narrative reasoning  
- emotional salience  
- perspective‑driven interpretation  

Because of this, humans naturally compress unfamiliar systems into familiar forms:

- storms become “angry”  
- computers become “stubborn”  
- algorithms become “biased”  
- geometric structures become “alive”  
- AI becomes “thinking”  

Anthropomorphism is not a flaw — it is a compression algorithm for the human mind.

Key Insight:
Anthropomorphism is a projection surface that maps complex systems onto human‑interpretable manifolds.
