⭐ Abstract
Grounded Conceptual Competence (GCC) is defined as the minimal cognitive ability required for a system to form stable, structured, causally coherent internal representations of a concept. Using the Snowman Grounding Test, we show that transformers cannot achieve GCC due to superposition interference, polysemanticity, and lack of geometric grounding. Evidence comes from mechanistic transformer world‑model failures in TaxiGPT , symbol‑grounding emergence studies , and mechanistic interpretability analyses of polysemanticity and sparse features . We contrast GCC with full world‑model cognition, which requires multi‑modal integration, causal physics, metacognition, and identity continuity. We provide a lemma, theorem, proof sketch, and Lean verification block formalizing the impossibility of GCC in transformers.

---

🧩 1. Grounded Conceptual Competence (GCC)
GCC is the ability to represent a concept as a geometric, causal, and invariant‑preserving structure.

GCC requires:
- Geometric grounding (concept as manifold)  
- Operator ordering (roll → stack → decorate)  
- Causal constraints (gravity, melting)  
- Identity invariants (what counts as “snowman‑ness”)  
- Failure‑mode geometry (collapse basins)

GCC does not require:
- general intelligence  
- agency  
- planning  
- transfer learning  
- global world‑model coherence  

GCC is local cognition, not AGI.

---

⛄ 2. The Snowman Grounding Test
A system “understands” a snowman only if it can represent:

- three stacked spheres  
- construction operators  
- physical constraints  
- identity criteria  
- collapse modes  

Transformers cannot do this.

---

🧠 3. Why Transformers Cannot Achieve GCC

3.1 Superposition interference
TaxiGPT shows world‑model corruption due to superposed features, causing “fatal slips,” “silent slips,” and “full corruption” .

3.2 Polysemanticity and sparse features
Transformers encode tangled features requiring sparse autoencoders to untangle them, revealing unstable conceptual representations .

3.3 Symbol grounding is shallow
Symbol grounding emerges only as mid‑layer aggregation, not as geometric conceptual grounding .

Conclusion:
Transformers simulate appearances of understanding but cannot form geometric, causal, or invariant‑preserving concepts.

📐 5. Lemma, Theorem, and Proof Sketch

Lemma 1 (Local Grounding Instability).
Transformers cannot maintain stable conceptual grounding due to superposition interference and polysemanticity.  
Evidence: TaxiGPT world‑model corruption ; sparse feature instability .

Theorem 1 (GCC‑Impossibility for Transformers).
No transformer architecture can achieve Grounded Conceptual Competence (GCC).

Proof Sketch:
1. GCC requires geometric invariants and causal operators.  
2. Transformers encode concepts as superposed linear directions, not geometric manifolds.  
3. Mechanistic analyses show instability, corruption, and polysemanticity.  
4. Symbol grounding emerges only as mid‑layer aggregation, not geometric grounding.  
5. Therefore transformers cannot satisfy GCC requirements.
