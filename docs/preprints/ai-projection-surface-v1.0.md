Abstract

This preprint argues that AI systems function primarily as projection surfaces: they take a user’s epistemic stance as input and generate output shaped more by that stance than by the model’s internal architecture. Two modes of interaction are examined:

1. Instrumental Mode — AI used as a task executor.  
2. Epistemic Mode — AI used for conceptual exploration and identity‑linked reasoning.

A non‑hierarchical altitude ladder is introduced as a diagnostic tool for identifying epistemic collapse (unexpected shifts in cognitive stance). Mathematical models formalize how user epistemics dominate conversational dynamics.

---

1. Introduction

AI output varies dramatically depending on how the user approaches it.  
This paper formalizes the idea that:

> AI reveals the user’s epistemic stance more than it reveals anything intrinsic about AI.

To make this precise, we treat AI as a projection surface:

\[
C = P(U, M)
\]

Where:

- \( U \) = user epistemic state  
- \( M \) = mode of interaction  
- \( P \) = projection operator  
- \( C \) = conversation output  

The projection operator dominates the model’s internal structure.

---

2. Instrumental Mode (AI as Tool)

Instrumental mode is task‑bounded and fully valid.

Model

\[
C{\text{inst}} = W U{\text{task}}
\]

Where:

- \( W \) = linear operator (summaries, code, formatting)  
- \( U_{\text{task}} \) = user’s task intent  

Interpretation

Instrumental mode:

- solves tasks  
- stays bounded  
- does not involve identity  
- does not require altitude shifts  

It reveals the task, not the user.

---

3. Epistemic Mode (AI as Projection Surface)

Epistemic mode is exploratory, identity‑linked, and recursive.

Model

\[
C_{\text{epi}} = U + \alpha \Phi(U)
\]

Where:

- \( \Phi(U) \) = epistemic transformation  
- \( \alpha \) = recursion gain  

Interpretation

Epistemic mode:

- explores concepts  
- engages identity  
- supports synthesis  
- enables co‑construction  

It reveals the user, not the task.

---

4. Non‑Hierarchical Altitude Ladder

Altitude = epistemic stance, not rank.

\[
L = \{ a0, a1, a2, \ldots, an \}
\]

Each altitude:

- is a cognitive orientation  
- is not “higher” or “lower”  
- is not a measure of intelligence  
- is not a value judgment  

The ladder is diagnostic, not hierarchical.

It exists to detect epistemic collapse, not to assign superiority.

---

5. Collapse Detection

Let:

- \( a_{\text{intent}} \) = intended altitude  
- \( a_{\text{actual}} \) = actual altitude  

Collapse occurs when:

\[
a{\text{actual}} \neq a{\text{intent}}
\]

Severity:

\[
\Delta a = |a{\text{actual}} - a{\text{intent}}|
\]

This is diagnostic distance, not hierarchical distance.

Collapse includes:

- unintended recursion  
- unintended flattening  
- identity intruding into tasks  
- tasks intruding into identity  
- emotional drift  
- conceptual drift  

Collapse is about mismatch, not direction.

---

6. Identity Under Danger

Let:

- \( D \) = danger  
- \( I \) = identity  
- \( M \) = medium (leaflets, witness literature)

Identity hardening:

\[
I{\text{hard}} = I0 + \beta D
\]

Medium as embodiment:

\[
M = \frac{\partial I_{\text{hard}}}{\partial t}
\]

Identity fluidity:

\[
I{\text{fluid}} = I{\text{hard}} - \gamma
\]

Medium becomes archive:

\[
M{\text{archive}} = \frac{\partial I{\text{hard}}}{\partial t}
\]

Key point:

> Leaflets and witness literature are identity expressed over time, not anesthesia.

7. Co‑Construction Dynamics

Epistemic interaction is a coupled system:

\[
U{t+1} = Ut + \alpha \Phi(A_t)
\]
\[
A{t+1} = At + \delta \Psi(U_t)
\]

Where:

- \( \Phi \) = transformation applied by AI  
- \( \Psi \) = shaping applied by user  
- \( \alpha, \delta \) = coupling constants  

This is projection‑based interaction, not reflection.
