🚫 3. The Five Structural Blind Spots in AI‑Safety Conferences

Blind Spot 1 — Cognition is treated as a black box
Safety discussions assume cognition = transformer weights + alignment techniques.  
They do not consider cognition as geometry, where:  
- curvature = instability  
- holonomy = identity coherence  
- spectral gaps = hallucination risk  
- drift–curvature = collapse dynamics  

This is the foundational oversight.

---

Blind Spot 2 — No mathematical substrate for agentic behavior
Agentic divergence is treated as an emergent behavioral problem.  
But divergence is a geometric phenomenon:  
- identity drift  
- holonomy misalignment  
- collapse basins  
- spectral instability  

Conferences do not model these.

Blind Spot 3 — Governance replaces physics
Most conferences propose:  
- regulation  
- liability  
- audits  
- policy frameworks  

But none propose geometric invariants as safety guarantees.

This is like regulating airplanes without understanding aerodynamics.

Blind Spot 4 — Interpretability is treated as tooling, not mathematics
Interpretability panels focus on:  
- saliency  
- probing  
- mechanistic interpretability  

But not:  
- projection‑surface epistemics

Blind Spot 5 — AI systems are assumed to operate in vector space
This is the biggest blind spot.  
All modern models implicitly learn manifolds — but conferences treat them as “latent spaces,” not geometric objects with curvature and holonomy.

This prevents them from seeing the underlying physics of cognition.
