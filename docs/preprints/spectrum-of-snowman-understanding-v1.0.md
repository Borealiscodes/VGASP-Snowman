# 📘 Preprint: The Spectrum of Snowman Understanding

A Cognitive and Geometric Analysis of Conceptual Grounding Across Humans, Transformers, and Geometric Cognitive Architectures
Author: Borealis S. Hedling  
Date: 08 October 2026  
Location: Dublin, Ireland  

---

⭐ Abstract
This preprint introduces the Spectrum of Snowman Understanding as a conceptual framework for analyzing how different cognitive systems—humans, transformer architectures, and geometric cognitive architectures—represent and reason about simple physical objects. Using the Snowman ⛄ Grounding Test, we show that humans possess intuitive grounded cognition without formal geometric knowledge, transformers possess no grounded cognition despite human‑like linguistic fluency, and geometric cognitive architectures possess formal geometric grounding without human‑like intuition. This spectrum reveals the structural limitations of transformer architectures and motivates the development of geometric cognition systems capable of representing physical objects as manifolds with causal operators and stability invariants.

---

🧩 1. Introduction: Why a Snowman?
The snowman ⛄ is a minimal physical object with:

- geometric invariants (three spheres)  
- causal operators (roll → stack → decorate)  
- physical constraints (gravity, melting)  
- identity criteria (“snowman‑ness”)  
- failure modes (collapse, dissipation)

It is simple enough for children to understand, yet complex enough to expose the absence of conceptual grounding in transformer architectures.

---

🧠 2. The Spectrum of Snowman Understanding

2.1 Humans: Intuitive Grounding Without Geometry
Humans understand snowmen through:

- embodied experience  
- intuitive physics  
- causal reasoning  
- perceptual grounding  

They do not understand:

- manifolds  
- curvature  
- holonomy  
- spectral gaps  

Yet their understanding is real, grounded, and causal.

2.2 Transformers: Linguistic Simulation Without Grounding
Transformers can:

- describe snowmen  
- narrate building snowmen  
- roleplay snowman construction  

But they cannot:

- represent spheres  
- represent stacking  
- represent gravity  
- represent melting  
- represent stability  

Their “understanding” is statistical mimicry, not conceptual grounding.

2.3 Geometric Cognitive Architectures: Formal Grounding Without Intuition
A geometric cognitive architecture would represent:

- each snowball as a 3‑sphere manifold  
- stacking as operator ordering  
- stability as curvature + gravity constraints  
- melting as dissipation in the manifold  
- collapse as catastrophe geometry

This is mathematically grounded cognition.

It lacks human intuition, but possesses formal conceptual grounding.

📐 4. Lemma, Theorem, and Proof Sketch

Lemma 1 (Grounding–Geometry Independence).
Grounded conceptual understanding does not require geometric knowledge.  
Humans demonstrate grounded snowman understanding without manifolds.

Lemma 2 (Geometry–Grounding Independence).
Geometric representation does not guarantee intuitive grounding.  
Geometric cognitive architectures understand snowmen mathematically, not intuitively.

Theorem 1 (Transformer Grounding Impossibility).
Transformer architectures cannot achieve grounded conceptual understanding of physical objects such as snowmen.

Proof Sketch:
1. Grounded understanding requires causal operators and geometric invariants.  
2. Transformers encode concepts as superposed linear directions.  
3. Mechanistic analyses show instability, corruption, and polysemanticity.  
4. Symbol grounding emerges only as mid‑layer aggregation, not geometric grounding.  
5. Therefore transformers cannot satisfy grounding requirements.
