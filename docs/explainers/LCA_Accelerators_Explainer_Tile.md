# 🧪 Bill Nye Tile — “Why Your GPU Has a Carbon Shadow” (APA‑cited, accessible)

Hey science fans!  
Let’s talk about something surprising: your AI accelerator has a carbon shadow, and it’s way bigger than the electricity it uses.

1. Mining: The Hidden Mountain Behind Every GPU
To build AI datacentres, we need a lot of copper — 83% of all minerals required through 2035 (Amoah, Li, & Hernandez, 2026).  
That means:

- giant open‑pit mines  
- diesel trucks the size of houses  
- tailings ponds that can stretch for kilometers  

Example:  
A single large datacentre expansion can require thousands of tons of copper, enough to fill several shipping containers.

---

2. Manufacturing: The HBM Explosion
HBM (High‑Bandwidth Memory) is the real carbon heavyweight.

TechInsights (2025) shows:

- Manufacturing emissions rise from 1.8 million tons CO₂e (2025)  
- to 21.6 million tons CO₂e (2030)  
- because HBM dies per accelerator jump from ~40 → ~250

Example:  
Think of HBM like stacking pancakes.  
In 2024 you had a short stack.  
By 2030 you have a skyscraper.

And every “pancake” costs energy, chemicals, and water.

---

3. Transport: Around the World in 80,000 Kilometers
Chips travel through Taiwan → China → global hubs, using:

- air freight (high emissions)  
- container ships (lower per kg, but long distances)  
- trucks (last‑mile delivery)

(CPAI Education, 2024)

Example:  
A GPU’s components may travel farther than you will in your entire life.

---

4. Operation: The Electricity You Do See
Datacentres consumed 415 TWh in 2024 (International Energy Agency, 2024).

But here’s the twist:

> Manufacturing and mining now outweigh operational electricity.

This is the part most people miss.

---

5. The Big Problem
If we keep scaling AI the same way, emissions follow a superlinear curve — faster than exponential.

By 2032–2035, the system becomes ecologically unsustainable.

🌍 Attenborough Explainer — “The Quiet Cost of Intelligence” (APA‑cited, accessible)

In the quiet places of our world — the copper valleys of Chile, the forests of Indonesia — the story of artificial intelligence begins.

The Earth Beneath the Circuits
Copper, the lifeblood of electrical infrastructure, forms the vast majority of minerals required for AI datacentres (Amoah et al., 2026).  
Its extraction reshapes landscapes and ecosystems.

Example:  
A single copper mine can move hundreds of millions of tons of earth over its lifetime.

---

The Furnace of Creation
In Taiwan, fabrication plants glow through the night.  
TSMC alone consumed 25.55 TWh in 2024 (TSMC, 2024), a figure rivaling the electricity use of entire nations.

HBM memory rises in shimmering stacks — from ~40 dies to ~250 dies per accelerator by 2030 (TechInsights, 2025).

Example:  
If each HBM die were a book, a 2024 GPU would be a small shelf.  
A 2030 GPU would be a library tower.

---

Journeys Across the World
These components travel great distances — by air, sea, and road — through supply chains spanning continents (CPAI Education, 2024).

Example:  
A single accelerator may cross three oceans before reaching its datacentre.

---

The Datacentre: A New Habitat
Here, accelerators hum with purpose.  
They learn, infer, and generate.  
Their electricity consumption contributes to the 415 TWh used by datacentres in 2024 (IEA, 2024).

Yet this is no longer the greatest part of their impact.

---

A Curve That Rises Too Fast
If we continue on our present path, emissions will grow superlinearly.

By 2032, manufacturing will overshadow all other impacts.  
By 2035, the system may reach a point from which recovery becomes difficult.
